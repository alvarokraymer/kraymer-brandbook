Kraymer Art — Brand Book

One system,
not a template.

Kraymer Art exists between two worlds: artisan high-end jewelry and anime culture. This document gathers the creative principles that connect every launch, campaign and customer touchpoint into one recognisable identity.

Based on the brand's creative direction framework. The brand-audit sections (current state, perceived-value erosion) have been left out of this operating guide — this document covers the execution system.

01Foundations

Brand expression

The identity should preserve the craftsmanship, materiality and permanence of jewelry while carrying the emotion, symbolism and recognition that make fandom meaningful. It should never feel like traditional luxury, generic minimalism or literal merchandise.

Product first

The product is always the visual focal point

Every asset is subordinate to the piece. Nothing competes with the product.

Clarity

One primary message

Every asset should communicate one primary message, without added noise.

Consistency

Systems over individual decisions

Systems take priority over one-off individual creative decisions per project.

Craftsmanship

Materials, finishes and details should always be visible

The technical detail of the piece must always be perceptible.

Subtle fandom

References should reward recognition, not demand it

References reward those who recognise them; they're never forced on those who don't.

02Creative process

The creative workflow

Every launch follows the same process — not because every collection is the same, but because every collection deserves the same level of creative thinking.

01

Creative Concept

One central idea — a character, world, symbol or emotion that justifies the collection before anything is produced.

02

Creative Direction

The world is built around the idea — mood, references, symbols, colour — before deciding how any single asset should look.

03

Asset Planning

The creative vision is translated into a production roadmap: which assets, in what order, with what priority.

04

Execution

Photography, CGI, motion, design and content production — all answering the same brief.

Inspiration, not replication

Anime is the starting point of every collection, never the final outcome. Kraymer Art does not recreate characters or scenes — it translates the ideas, emotions and symbolism behind them into collectible jewelry.

Inspiration over replicationEmotion before characterSymbolism before illustrationCollectibility before noveltyCraftsmanship elevates the reference
03Creative process

Collection World

Every collection has its own Collection World: a dedicated document that defines the visual and emotional direction of the launch before any production begins. It must be developed before photography, CGI, video, campaign design or social production starts.

  1. 01
    Core Idea — one sentence that defines the central concept of the collection.
  2. 02
    Mood — the emotional atmosphere the collection should communicate.
  3. 03
    References — visual, cultural and artistic references that inform the direction.
  4. 04
    Symbols — recurring elements, details or motifs connected to the collection.
  5. 05
    Colour — the primary palette and supporting accents.
  6. 06
    Creative Applications — how the world should translate into photography, CGI, video, web and social content.
  7. 07
    Do's & Don'ts — a concise definition of what belongs within the world and what moves away from it.
04Creative process

Creative disciplines

Every discipline translates the Collection World into its own language, always under the same principles.

Product visuals

The product is always the subject. Accuracy is never sacrificed for aesthetics. Materials, proportions and finishes must remain faithful to the real product.

Graphic design

Typography communicates hierarchy, never decoration. Layouts should feel clean, balanced and intentional.

Motion

Motion should support the story, not distract from it. It should feel intentional and restrained.

Digital experience

Storytelling supports conversion. Navigation should feel effortless — the product remains the focus.

Storytelling

Every collection communicates one clear narrative, built through symbolism and atmosphere — never exposition.

09Visual identity

Composition

Composition determines how information is prioritised before it is read. Consistent layouts reduce visual friction and create familiarity.

One focal point

Every asset has one dominant element: the product, the campaign image, the collection title or the central message.

Image dominance

Practical hierarchy: Image → Headline → Supporting information → Action. Never give equal visual weight to every element.

Scale & space

Space isolates the product, increases perceived value, separates information and directs the eye. Empty areas should feel intentional.

10Voice & content

Copywriting

Language should feel as intentional as the visual identity. Clear, restrained and confident writing strengthens recognition.

Product descriptions

Craftsmanship before adjectives. Explain what the piece is, how it is made and what inspired it.

Collection stories

Narrative before catalogue. Introduce the world behind the collection, not the list of products within it.

Calls to action

Guidance before urgency. No artificial pressure or fake countdowns.

Brand language

Precision before promotion. Choose words that describe, not words that simply try to impress.

Tone

Confidence before persuasion. State things clearly. Let the work speak for itself.

Headlines

Short before descriptive. Capture the idea in a few words; let supporting copy provide the detail.

13Channels

Digital experience

Design for mobile first. Decisions around hierarchy, navigation and interaction should always prioritise the smallest viewport.

Mobile first

Every decision is made for the small viewport first, then expanded.

Predictable navigation

Users should never need to relearn how to move between collections, products or content.

Reusable components

Interfaces built from components rather than unique page designs.

Non-negotiable rule: text must be live, selectable text — never baked into an image.

14Channels

Social media

Social media functions as an extension of the brand, not a separate channel. Branding remains subtle — avoid persistent logos, frames or watermarks.

Formats

4:5 for feed · 9:16 for stories/reels · 1:1 only when required. Design for the native format, never crop existing assets.

Feed structure

Balanced mix of product, campaign, storytelling and community content — avoid long sequences of the same post type.

Asset reuse

Every execution should generate assets across feed, stories, reels, website, advertising and email before creating new content.

15Channels

Motion

Motion adds rhythm and emphasis without becoming the focus itself. It should reinforce hierarchy, reveal information and communicate interaction.

Timing

Short, deliberate transitions — precision over spectacle.

Movement

Scale, opacity and position favoured over exaggerated effects or complex transformations.

System

Similar interactions move in similar ways, regardless of platform or campaign.

16Channels

Advertising

Advertising is often the first interaction people have with Kraymer Art. Every campaign should both reinforce the brand and promote a product or collection, creating recognition.

17Operations

Asset management

Creative assets are long-term brand resources, not disposable production files.

Organisation

One source before multiple versions. Every approved asset has a single master file.

Structure

Systems before folders. Organised by collection, campaign and format.

Naming

Provisional format: Collection_AssetType_Platform_Version

Scalability

Reuse before recreation. Approved source files stay editable; exports are generated from those masters.

18Operations

Review & quality control

No asset is published without final approval from the Creative Director. Every piece is evaluated against the full framework before going into production.
